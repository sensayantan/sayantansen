Time to have Indian coach for Team Cricket India and there is no one better that Sourav Ganguli

By Sayantan Sen, January 01, 2015.

While WC 2015 is just round the corner and as usual expectations are very high, authors also feels that it is the time to think beyond Duncan Fletcher, current India coach for Team India whose tenure will end post 2015 ICC Cricket WC  .



BCCI is cash rich, and it shows off when it comes towards identifying the coach and support stuff of Indian Cricket Team. Never seems to be looking at desi talent. Why? I think only BCCI will know why and may be someone like Ravi Shastri and/or Sunil Gavaskar (who for some strange reasons always are in the good books of BCCI as they are always consulted before identifying the all important India Cricket Team's coach-cum-manager). Looking of foreign coach seems to be too predictable now and there is absolutely no reason to pay such a bomb as part of each such contracts, especially when ultimately the excess of cash that BBCI is paying is India citizens and corporate's money. Why not look for India coach, when there is so much of talent?

  • Who was the coach of India Team when T20 WC was won? There was none actually, and Lalchand Rajput was appointment the intermediate manager. Was he not successful?
  • Transformation of Kings XI Punjab in IPL, 2014 was under the leadership of which manager? It was Sanjay Banger. Was he not successful?
  • Who was the coach of India Team when 1983 Prudential WC was won? PR Man Singh, an official of the Hyderabad Cricket Association was just the manager and there was no coach appointed. Was Team India not successful?

Football or Hockey (unlike game of Cricket)requires a lots of inputs from the coach as in such fast paced game, strategy and planning plays a very significant role. There are reasons for game like hockey or soccer to have a foreign coach especially for an country like India as they have limited international exposures and knowledge of best practices.  But just like individual sports like Lawn tennis, Golf or Badminton, cricket as a game lot depends on individual players performance and implementing the right tactics for the game. It is more of a players game, rather than strategists game. For an example, India Badminton is at its best phase due to the right influence from Indian origin coach. It is time India cricket team should have a "India" coach/manager too.

India as a cricketing nations is one among the best. India has legands in all departments. We have instances where Indian cricket coach has done tremendously well. Ajit Wadekar between 1992 thru 1996 was very successful and so was to an certain extend Ansuman Gaekward during the period of 1997 - 1999. BCCI tried with Kapil Dev between 199-2000 and was not enough successful. Since then Team India had foreign coaches.
 John Wright (2000–2005) - John was hand-picked up by Sourav himself. John was quite successful and it was almost known that during his tenure Sourav almost made all the decisions.
Greg Chappell (2005–2007) - Greg was considered the worst coarch ever to have played that role and his tenure was almost a dark phase of Indian cricket.
 Gary Kirsten (2007–2011) - Gary was the most successful coach till now and his partnership with Dhoni/Kumble was phenomenal.
Duncan Fletcher (2011– 2015 (probably)) - Duncan in no means can be stated to be anywhere close to be successful.

Just going by overall success measure, 2 out of 4 such foreign coach have been successful till now, which is barely 50%.  Not at all impressive.

From author's perspective some of the characteristics that Indian coach should have are:
(1) An individual who has been immensely successful as a player himself in the international arena. This is something which goes along with Indian culture and mode of up-bringing. Indian only believes on someone who was a role model at some-point. There are nations (especially first-world countries) who believes in specialists and knows and accepts the fact that specialists might not be as successful as a player himself/herself. In USA NBA league, we have seen even female coach being appointed in an otherwise completely male dominated NBA league (Becky Hammon is first female assistant coach for San Antonio Spurs). That is why coach like Dave Whatmore or Russell Domingo (current SA coach) might not be right choice for Indian cricket team ever and they themselves not that successful cricketer. Duncan Fletcher falls in that similar category and was very surprised with he being appointment as the India coach.
(2)  An individual who is contemporary and knows almost all the right tricks in the game of cricket. This is something that is necessary for competitive advantage. Appointing a coach who has played cricket in a different generation might not be very successful. Was that the reason why Kapil Dev was not so successful (1999-2000)? Coach himself should not be behind from the thinking and strategy perspective and have the ability to know how the other teams behave and think.
(3)  An individual who has the ability to understand Indian way of thinking and knows to influence the team and management the right way. This point again almost goes hand-in-hand with the first point. It is all to do with Indian culture and sentiments. India believes in lord worship. India believes in following the leader. India believes following the power center. So an coach should know how to influence the senior members towards achieving greater heights and not making them the demi-gods, a coach who can influence the youngsters and last but not the least a coach can overcome board favors and pressure tactics and is able to bring in the right talent for the best interest of Indian cricket.
(4) Should know how officials work and have the experience of rubbing shoulders with them for a significant period of time. The best bet for this experience to have, is someone who have either captained the national side for significant period of time (as an captain, to need to play dual role always) or is been a power center and have/had strong influence among the officials for a significant period of time.


Looking  at these characteristics, while there are many many candidates, it is desired that someone who has tremendous respect as a leader should be given this opportunity first and there is no better person that Sourav Ganguli.
Some of the great features that suits Sourav for this positions are:
  • Sourav as a captain has changed the face of Indian cricket forever. He performed with great regularity and hence always lead by example. You name any cricketer who came as a youngster and made big in international circuit, Sourav has been influential towards his growth. Yuvraj, Harbhajan, Zaheer, MS Dhoni, Verendra Sehwag, Gautam Gambhir, Ashish Nehra, Suresh Raina and even some of the players like Mohammed Kaif, Parthiv Patel, Dinesh Karthik, Dinesh Mongia who failed to make big in the international circuit. Hence he carries a great respect among all.
Sourav as a captain always was able to extract the best out of each individuals. Under his leadership Sachin hit his peak and his friendship, respect and admiration with/towards Sachin  is a great Jai-Veru story. Under his leadership Dravid transformed from a Test Batsman to one of the greatest one-day batsman too.  Dravid's presence in 2003 ICC WC was somewhat questionable to some section, but at the same time Sourav knew how important Dravid was for the team. To make the right team balance and enhance Dravid's confidence he deployed Dravid as a wicket keeper in the team and everybody knows how successful the ploy was. He was a great strategists...
He as a captain and individuals has also taken some tough calls which otherwise would have been very difficult under regular circumstances (especially from Indian emotional view-point). He dropped Kumble from the playing 11 during 2003 ICC WC (to increase batting depth) and dropped himself to the middle-order as Viru-Sachin was more lethal (inspite of Sachin-Sourav was the all-time greatest one-day opening pair and one had to sacrifice the ever lucrative right-left batting combination) in the opening positions.
  • Sourav as a captain and player has seen tremendous ups and downs. From being ICC Batsman number one for more than 3 years, leading Team Indian to 2003 ICC WC finals, leading Team India to 2004 and 2006 ICC Champions trophy finals to being dropped from India Team under Greg Chapells coaching tenure and then again making a comeback to India Team after 14 months. What a journey. Sourav knows the joy and the pain of playing the game of cricket at the highest level and knows how to handle pressure for long enough period (14+ years of international cricket).
  • Sourav as an administrator and thinker is greatly admired. Some of the few such glimpses were
                (A) He was part of the Supreme Court of India appointed Justice Mudgal Committee probe panel for the       IPL Spot fixing and betting scandal's investigations
                (B) Charismatic Prime Minister Sri. Narendra Modi chose him (along with 6 other important Indian personalities and corporates) to take forward the "Swachata BharatAbhiyan".
                (C) He was awarded with the Banga Bibhushan Award from the Government of West Bengal on 20 May         2013
                (D) Along with Harshavardhan Neotia, Sanjiv Goenka, Utsav Parekh, and Spanish La Liga side Atletico              Madrid, Ganguly is also a co-owner of Atlético de Kolkata, a franchise of the Indian Super League. Under                his charismatic influence Atlético de Kolkata was the champion for the first edition of ISL (Indian Soccer               League) in 2014.
  • During Sourav's ugly  episode with Greg Chappel, it was never observed Sourav to be vocal against Greg (although it was he who picked Greg for the job and was the captain of India cricket team), on the contrary Greg made the episode more public and media-hungry. Even during the period when he was dropped and was struggling to make a comeback, inspite of many temptations thrown infront of him (by media etc), he never chose to speak a single word against Greg or the dark phase team India was going through. He made a tough comeback and made his exit at the right time (July, 2008 with a final series batting average of 54.00 and 1 century in his second last test).

He is undoubtedly the best pick for Indian cricket team manager/coach position, especially when team India will again be in some sort of a building phase with all the last set of stall-wards hanged their boot or will soon hang their boots (Yuvraj, Harbhajan, Zaheer, MS Dhoni, Verendra Sehwag, Gautam Gambhir etc) and new age aggressive team India under the leadership of Virat Kohli (probably in all formats soon) will have to place its mark in world cricket again. The timing and the context is right for Sourav to excel and show all that "desi kisi se kum nehi".....
