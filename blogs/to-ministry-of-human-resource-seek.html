October 30, 2014

Seek equal opportunity

This Children's day, a day which is celebrated for any nation's future, the young generation. A future of hope, prospect, growth and a better Country I want to share a view-point which is very close to heart. 
India as a country is very diverse and with population of un-imaginable proportion competition among the peers is obvious. Competition for better opportunity, better livelihood and better future. Unfortunately this environment gets into the eco-system of young children very early and hence it is often observed that a child is no longer a child. They don't cry, just screams, they don't laugh just smiles, they don't explore just plays. They get into adult mode very early. In this rat race eventually smart kids become smarter and the weak ones gets weaker. Columnist hence emphasizes that  this society only worships the success and not much the triers and laggers. But is it not a societies responsibility to embrace everyone? Yes current economy works on the simple principle of demand and supply and survival of the fittest, but is that humanity all about. Should society not be more tolerant, more expressive to support the poor and weaker section? Please note that the ask is not to show empathy, but to be more embracing.

Yes, I am talking here of children with various disabilities. These gifted children have not come to this world with such condition on their wish, neither with their parent's wish. God has gifted them this condition and with the hope that each such beautiful soul would grow just like anyone, but with little bit of more support so that eventually gain the courage to fight the condition and help make themselves independent. But unfortunately this struggle and fight is a lonely one. Fighting alone in this ever changing world of rat race, it getting increasingly difficult. When the columnist tried to get some data points on what Ministry of Human Resource has provided for necessary support, there are lot many Acts and Laws. All are very encouraging, but unfortunately far from what the reality is. For an example for condition like Autism, with around 1 out of 100 children having this condition there are hand few specialized  school to help these children, while a country like Germany provides similar support to these condition till the level of university.

Not going to any kind of comparison, what is the need of the hour is institutionalize the policies, laws and acts across schools, colleges (govt., private) so that (1) A consistency is achieved across when a child with special need approaches these institution (2) Tailor and provide more structured alternative schooling curriculum (including Home schooling) which understands the need to various disabilities and as well as is accepted well [Indian education system still considers Home schooling as something not globally accepted and is only meant to perform some vocational activities and not to do regular jobs] (3) Have adequate resources, funding available to help them complete their education as per each of the child's ability without any limit. Currently on the ground, the experience of having just elementary education for these special need children is so inconsistent and almost so traumatic that parents are almost forced to accept that they are here to struggle and will only be empathized and nothing else. Why special needs children will be told to look out for special school only? Why they are been directed to start vocational training at early age? Are they not allowed play and dream along with others. Why they are not allowed to sit in a class with all? Yes, they need extra support, guidance, counseling and why this society and education institution in particular will not take that responsibility?

Any great country as they mature also goes to societal maturity, acceptance and broad mindedness. Columnist is definitely of the opinion that people still here in India has to go a long way. Columnist at this juncture wants Ministry to drive a country wise initiative towards stricter adherence of existing laws and policies, similar like what is getting carried out for "Government School without Toilets" (http://mhrd.gov.in/). To build this consistency and accountability, each state chapters need to be made more active, execution oriented and people oriented. Just like RTI Act, these should be some forum, platform to take such grievances to the right platform. Such transparency and lesser bureaucracy would make schools and educational institutions think twice before letting down a child's birth right. Teachers and professionals who have chosen to help and guide special needs children, should be incentivized adequately so that they don't end up into glorified private business entity and make themselves only accessible to the riches. Every large nation has to face this reality and the nations who actually  are more open, structure and organized to such conditions are later on blessed with geniuses (not to name anyone here who eventually make their nation and society proud), not just their near and dear ones who anyhow know how blessed they are to get this opportunity to be with a special need kid.
