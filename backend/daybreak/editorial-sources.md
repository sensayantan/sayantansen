# Daybreak editorial sources and synthesis policy

This file is the required source roster and editorial workflow for every Daybreak run. It complements `scheduled-task.md`; it is not a claim that every publisher will always be reachable. The scheduled researcher must attempt the relevant roster for every desk, record access failures in the private research audit, and never imply that a paywalled, blocked, unavailable, or unindexed page was read.

## Required workflow for every editorial desk

1. **Set the edition window.** Use the current Pacific date and the repository's freshness rules. Load the prior working edition only as a list of continuing-event leads.
2. **Sweep the complete desk roster.** Visit each reachable publisher's current top-news or relevant regional page. Capture candidate headline, article URL, publisher, publication/update time, geography, event, and the factual claims supported by that article. Do not mark a source as reviewed merely because it appeared in search results.
3. **Record coverage limitations.** In the private research audit, label publishers that were blocked, paywalled, unavailable, not updated, or not machine-readable. Continue with the rest of the roster and authoritative local or official sources. Never weaken a factual claim to conceal an inaccessible source.
4. **Translate when needed.** Prefer a publisher's English edition. If no English version exists and the page is readable, translate the relevant reporting into English at runtime. Preserve names, dates, numbers, uncertainty, and attribution; do not translate commentary into fact. Link the original-language article and label its language.
5. **Normalize and cluster events.** Assign a stable event ID and group articles describing the same real-world event, even when headlines or languages differ. Treat syndicated copies of one wire story as one source, not independent corroboration.
6. **Corroborate claims.** Every published story needs at least two genuinely independent source domains. For casualty counts, court outcomes, government action, research findings, market values, or disputed claims, prefer an official or primary source plus independent reporting when available. Attribute unresolved differences explicitly.
7. **Rank candidate events.** Rank by verified human impact, urgency, scale, public consequence, geographic reach, and material newness. A major disaster or mass-casualty event can move into Top News regardless of geography. Top News is selected first; the same event must not appear again in a regional desk.
8. **Synthesize neutrally.** Derive a factual, non-sensational headline. Write a neutral synthesis no longer than 200 words using the combined evidence, not one article's framing. Separate established facts, attributed allegations, forecasts, and analysis. Do not invent connective facts or causal explanations.
9. **Link the evidence.** Attach every article materially used to derive the headline or synthesis, subject to the renderer's source limits. Links must point to original publishers when accessible. Aggregator or syndicated links must be labelled accurately.
10. **Revisit continuing stories if below target.** If the desk remains below its soft target, search yesterday's event list for material current-window updates such as a new official decision, verified impact change, result, filing, escalation, recovery milestone, or resolution. Build the updated story solely from newly retrieved evidence. A recap, rewritten headline, commentary, or unchanged background does not qualify.
11. **Apply quality gates.** Enforce the desk maximum, two-domain corroboration, freshness, valid timestamps, source verification timestamps, and cross-section deduplication. The minimum count remains a soft target: publish fewer stories and disclose the shortfall rather than padding the desk.
12. **Audit before rendering.** Confirm that each summary is at most 200 words, every material numerical claim appears in cited evidence, all used links are present, translations are labelled, duplicate events are removed, and unavailable sources were not represented as read.

## Disaster and mass-casualty priority

The worldwide sweep must explicitly look for earthquakes, tsunamis, typhoons and hurricanes, floods, landslides, wildfires, mass shootings, massacres, terrorism, major accidents, and humanitarian emergencies. A candidate normally warrants Top News consideration when verified reporting shows one or more of the following:

- five or more confirmed deaths;
- substantial numbers missing, injured, displaced, or ordered to evacuate;
- extensive damage to homes, hospitals, transport, utilities, or other critical infrastructure;
- a highest-level official emergency warning or declaration;
- record-setting or exceptional physical severity; or
- a material current update to a major event from the prior edition.

These are priority signals, not permission to publish unverified numbers. Confirm impact with two independent domains and, where possible, a local authority, meteorological agency, emergency service, court, regulator, journal, or other primary source.

## Independence, balance, and source character

- Two articles repeating the same Reuters, AP, AFP, Bloomberg, Kyodo, or other wire copy count as one underlying source.
- Opinion pages, tabloids, partisan outlets, and state-funded outlets may contribute reported facts, but their framing must not be adopted without corroboration. Label opinion, analysis, and unverified claims.
- A large list of links does not itself make a story neutral. Neutrality comes from reconciling independently reported facts, representing material disagreements, and avoiding unsupported conclusions.
- Paywalled text must not be reconstructed from snippets. Cite a paywalled article only when enough of it was actually accessible to verify the claim.
- The report must never claim that the complete internet, every story, or every listed page was reviewed when access or indexing prevented that.
