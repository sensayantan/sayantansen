***By Sayantan Sen, January 01, 2015.***

  

*Post MS Dhoni's retirement from international Test Cricket, author wants
to know whether there was any lean period in his otherwise ever rising true "star
like" career.*

![](http://www.cricketcosmic.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/Mahendra-Singh-Dhoni.jpg)

MS Dhoni declared his retirement from
International Test Cricket. Everyone wants to comment on the timing of his
decision. Let's not discuss about the same more, as we are continuously hearing
about various speculations and stories around it and unless Dhoni himself
states something very explicit about this topic, let's not try to make it a
Ekhta Kapoor's Soap Opera of K-series....

Everybody is although acknowledging
MS as the greatest captain that this country has ever produced  and he was been the right candidate to take
the baton from Sourav Ganguli, who just like Dhoni was able to extract the best
out of the team always There are many many achievements for Dhoni.

* He has
  lead team India in 3 greatest world events as the champions, 2007 T20 WC,
  2011 WC and 2012 ICC Champion's trophy. Team India even came close for
  their 2nd T20 WC in 2014 when they lost to SL in the final. An achievement
  no-one can beat so easily.
* He has
  been instrumental in making India No. 1 in the ICC Test Rankings in
  December 2009, which as per even legends like Sachin Tendulkar was one of
  the greatest achievements for Team Cricket India. To be world number one
  in ICC test ranking, you have to perform reasonably well overseas also.
  Under his leadership, India won series in New-Zealand (2009), Bangladesh
  (2009/10), drew test series in Sri Lanka (2010), Drew Test Series in South
  Africa (2010/11), won in West Indies (2011) before starting to lose almost
  everything overseas.
* He has
  lead his franchisee Chennai Super Kings to two championship titles (2010,
  2011) and three runners-up tittles (2008, 2012, 2013, 2014) and the only
  team to have qualified for the play-off (semi-finals) in all eight
  editions of this very glamorous and highly competitive star-studded
  competition. He is the rock-star of Chennai Super Kings and is the biggest
  start for the IPL tournament. He is definitely second to Rajini when we
  talk of stardom in Chennai.

Most of Dhoni's not so great
achievements were in Test cricket. While there were obvious string of overseas
defeats in Test for Team India under his leadership for last three years across
the world (*England (twice), South Africa,
New Zealand, Australia (twice, if we take this series too)*), this low is
something which Dhoni cannot be blamed for. We are yet to find an Indian
captain who would potentially lead the team to perform better overseas and win
series with more confidence (*which
typically Australia and South Africa has done repeatedly*) . Dhoni at-least
have few memorable wins abroad (best pick was the win at Lord's against a
strong English side for the first time in 28 years, Jul'14),  which he and his team can boast of.  In between Dhoni has almost won all home
series except one (A rare embarrassment for Dhoni and India at home as England
beat them 2-1 in a four-Test series. It was India's first home Test series loss
in 28 years.).

If we ignore Dhoni's overseas
captaincy performance for  the sake of
argument, is there any other low point in Dhoni's cricketing career? Author
doesn't find much. Some of the great characters which makes Dhoni so different
and unique from any other contemporary cricketers are:

***(1)*** ***His leadership and performance has always
spoken aloud***.

He is the first cricketer of India
origin who has won matches single handedly in one-dayers and T20 cricket. His
hitting and completely changing his approach towards batting post captaincy
towards a more sheet-anchor role is regarded very high. His aggressive game
plans of rotating strikes, converting twos-to-three, placing un-orthodox
fields, changing batting and bowling orders  has wowed all cricketing pandits and analyzers.

He has also saved many test matches
from the jaws of defeat, the classical one was the last-man partnership to save
the test match against England in Jul'2007 (*scoring
76 not out off 159 balls while shepherding a sagging tail*). His not so
great Test batting form was ever felt primarily for his great charismatic and
composed leadership skill and performed when was most needed (in his last test
also, he stamped his presence with an unbeaten partnership with R Ashwin and
helping India to draw the test). Author feels that he is only one of the very
few cricket captain in the history of international cricket who will be more
remembered first as a captain and then as a player. I think one such individual
who can be compared with is Douglas Jardine, (*who is remembered for his body-line tactics in 1932-33*) one who
changed the game from thinking and strategy perspective only and is only
remembered as a captain (lead 15 tests out of 22 tests that he played at
international level). ***Leading by example.... always...***

(2) Indian Captains has been
typically over-exposed in media (this is what the job demands) and unlike some
of his predecessors (*Dravid, Kumble,
Sachin were trying not to be exposed while Ganguli was always very open and
over-reactive*) ***he played a very balanced role and his reaction from massive defeats or
spectacular wins has always been in moderation and hence has been able to
manage the media and external world very gracefully***. Not sure how he
managed his emotions so well. ***Flawless...***

(3) ***He seems to be a very much a team
man***. We keep on hearing how he has never ever talked for himself in
team selections, how he has supported the team members (*towards giving more chances*), team support stuff (*towards extending their tenure (proposal to
retain India bowling coach Joe Dawes and former fielding coach Trevor Penney
till the end of their contract period)*) etc. It is also observed how
non-reactive he has been on the field when some team members failed to perform
as per plan/expectations. Whenever there are dropped catches, or bowlers not
bowling as per the plan, we have hardly seen Dhoni reacting. When any cricketer
is playing at this level, he knows the impact of any mistake and just like a
great leader, Dhoni has given all individuals the required space. Dhoni exactly
knew how to deal with such situations. Epitome of such matured approach was observed
during the post match press conference for T20 2014 WC final. Media was very
critical of Yuvraj's performance (batting performance in that match) and any other
captain would have reacted differently in response to those allegations. He
chose to respond in a matter which a person with great heart and integrity,
respect for others and can only do. He mentioned that we should not be too
critical of Yuvraj as he is only one Indian cricket who has won two world cups
for his country almost single handedly and failure are part of any cricketers
career. In another such incident of being a great human being, he In the Fourth
and final Test of Sourav Ganguli (July, 2008), with India needing one wicket to
secure a victory, invited Ganguly to lead the side in the field for the final
time. India regained the Border-Gavaskar Trophy, winning the series 2–0. ***Salute
you....***

So do Dhoni has any drawbacks, any
dark/grey spots. There is hardly apart from one may be. His string of actions
to N Srinivasan's involvement in IPL spot-fixing has let lots of cricket lovers
disappointed. As expected lots of speculations and rumors are around and will continue
to float unless court decides to take any further decisive action on whom so
ever is necessary. What really disappoints cricket enthusiasts is why did Dhoni
decided to take this path. First he tried to cover up Gurunath Mayappan (*stated on Mar'14 by BCCI counsel CA Sundaram
"I think that comments on Indian captain Mahendra Singh Dhoni, that he
tried to cover up, were most unfortunate, were reckless and were never ought to
have been made"*) which raised a lot of eye-brows and then when N
Srinivasan was questioned repeatedly about conflict of interest along with
Dhoni himself, he decided to be very silent. Has this episode has not tarnished
Dhoni's image? ***May be yes..... May be the only one....***

It is always believed that that
"nothing is late than ever".  All individuals do make mistakes and sometimes
choices are incorrect. That doesn't make individuals wrong as long as he
decides to accept such circumstances. May be in due course of time Dhoni will
clean all those cob-webs and stamp his name not only in the book of history of
cricket as the greatest and the best, but also as a brand ambassador with great
ethics &  adequate behavior.
