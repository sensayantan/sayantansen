This ICC Cricket World Cup 2023 has brought a lot of joy and aspirations among billions of Indian cricket fans across the world. Arguably this year's India cricket team might have been the best performing team among all participating, the result unfortunately was not something that each one of us were praying for. Yes, there is no doubt in each of our minds that this India team has just given everything to make it happen, but it was just not that day (19-Nov-2023). Throughout my life, I have been following cricketing ICC events and have stuck to different superstition based strategies to support my team India (as a cricket fan, that is the only thing in my control). I remember in 2015, I ended up writing something or the other at the end of almost every India match on social media. In 2019 I only watched the second half of the matches. This time, although the timing was odd (*had to get up at 01:30 am in the middle of the night, or used to be awake till the game started at 12:30 am*), I watched all the matches alone sitting on the same couch everyday and calling my parents or my brother or sometimes friends in India during break for expert discussions with like minded folks. Every time, the same strategy worked until the most important day. When it mattered the most, superstition didn’t work. To my firm belief although any sporting event should be be most competitive and neutral in nature where everything is controllable or measurable and there is hardly any “X” factor which can make one team or one individual favorable to other beyond the basic skill quotient.I think in cricket there is one factor that dampens this neutrality factor immensely and it the PITCH FACTOR. Let me try to explain this with a few of the most popular sports across the world. 

* In Soccer almost everything is standardized. Toss doesn’t play any factor. The ball is largely standardized. Yes sometimes we do know that there are complaints about high altitude venues or high humid conditions etc which affects players performance and sometimes local teams have some favorable conditions, but that is too less of an impact I suppose. Professional footballers train themselves really hard to be capable of playing in all such conditions.
* Same kind of observations for any indoor sports like basketball, badminton etc. Everything is standardized. I do know that events like track & field may have some venue specific conditions which matters (say for a sprinter, the air speed matters, for a javelin thrower again the ais speed matters), but since all the players are competing at the same point in time (or almost same point in time) everyone is in the same playing condition.
* I think there are few sports where actually playing surfaces play a very crucial role. Tennis. Yes, tennis is a sport where playing surface (clay, grass or hard court) brings in different challenges to the players, and hence you will only find very few GOAT (Greatest of All Time) who are equally successful in all kinds of surfaces while you have champions for each type of surfaces. This is also a sport where you see specialists in each kind of surface. But in tennis, there is one great advantage. Each of the surfaces that are known for competitive professional tennis games are actually a known entity to every player. Not many surprises. Every event, every year you will get the same surface. A professional sportsman can train herself/himself for that surface and whoever displays better skill wins.
* Hockey was one such other sport (before the astro-turf era) where the surface played an important role and hence you will see countries like India or Pakistan as kind of undisputed champions at that time. An astro-turf surface broke any of those biases and whichever team had the ability to display their skill and adapt and train better to that astro-turf surface, would have won.

In cricket this PITCH FACTOR brings in a lot of variability along with some other uncontrollable factors like dew, toss (batting first vs. second), weather (rain, humidity) etc. Even though the Australian captain during the pre-match press conference claimed that he doesn’t rate himself to be a good pitch reader and India counterpart actually downplayed the pitch or the condition, we all know that the final result was largely to do with the pitch condition. There are a few rumors where the pitch was exposed to the sun intentionally to favor the local team which actually back-fired etc etc, but there is no doubt that Australian thinktank assessed the pitch better, extracted spongy kind of slower bounce to build that effect of fraction of a seconds slower response and to prevent the batsmen from natural batswing. 

What I want to enforce here is that, this is high-time we need to make this PITCH FACTOR a much more predictable and not an unknown, mystery element anymore. All time great Sachin Tendulkar does have some great ideas to make One Day cricket more competitive (each side batting twice 25 overs each etc) and I feel it also makes it less biased to any of the sides for sure.

Even though I am not an expert here, my wildest wish in order to make this game as neutral as possible and even more competitive  is to have a new technique called "PITCH WISDOM INDEX". With so much technical advancement, we should be able to standardize the pitch to a great extent. I believe every pitch can be measured on factors like Bounce, Spin, Swing, Damp, Grass Factor and maybe some others. Even if the core element of the pitch may be different in different parts of the world (*subcontinent soil is more RED soil, Australian solid may be more hard etc*), but again with more accurate monitoring and maintenance you (curators play a crucial role) can work towards proving a pitch a specific "PITCH WISDOM INDEX".

So under these these new rules of "PITCH WISDOM INDEX":

1. Every cricketing venue will have to commit to prepare pitches of "Major Grade" of A or B or C. Since each of the team will know well ahead what "Major Grade" a venue will offer (e.g. during the ICC cricketing hosting events, each team will exactly know that location X will provide "Major Grade" A pitch with location Y will provide Grade B pitch and no location will provide "Major Grade" C pitch), strategy team can prepare the team with “Major Grade” pitch category in mind. Countries hosting ICC events should be able to produce pitches of all kinds of "Major Grade" category. When each team has to announce their 14 or 15 players, going forward they will declare a team of 25 (maybe 30), because you will have 2 batters who play better in "Major Grade" A while you will have 3 other batters who will play better in "Major Grade" C. You actually take only 1 specialist wrist spinner in your team, because you will only play 1 game in the venue with "Major Grade" C, while the rest of the games are on "Major Grade" A venues.
2. One (1) day before the actual game of cricket the governing body will do the test to measure the exact "Minor Grading". Post that the pitch will be completely covered till the game day. No players or coaches will be allowed to look at the pitches (not needed actually) prior to the game.  As a Pre-Match press conference, I would love to see the match referee declare the "PITCH WISDOM INDEX" (e.g.: as B2) and then over the next 24 hours intense team strategy and planning takes place. Reporters and analysts started showing analysis of batters having different batting averages across A1 to C3 and how someone like World ranking 1 all-rounder might not find a place in the team. Each team's think tank will get to drawing boards to choose and announce their playing 15 (note not 11) just 12 hours before the game-day.
3. 2 hours before the match the toss will be done and depending on what the captain chooses to do, the team will submit their final playing 11, one (1) hour before the match. Strategy at its best. If (this is an Augmented Reality (AR) world that I am imagining 😆) the game rule really changes further (Sachin Tendulkar’s recommendation is applied and things like super-sub actually becomes legal)  may be team captains declare their playing 11 + 2 super-subs just 1 hour before the match.

  

All these  will allow the team's coach to play a much much bigger role. Just imagine listening to "PITCH WISDOM INDEX", you would actually would like to open your bowling with a spinner or open your batting having a strike rate of only 40 but a batting average of 80+ in "Major Grade" A venues

  

I don’t know what or how other cricket lovers would think or react (and I know that there can be loads of criticism around this) on these weird ideas, and definitely do know that real experts of this sports will think that all these are needed at all an in this sport it is a game of bat and ball and whoever has the greatest skil wins. But by point of view is this:

* When you have 2 teams like India, Australia playing where the gap in skill is really very minimal, any uncontrollable factors should be minimized to bring the element of strategy, element of preparedness into the game to the next level.
* I am not a very keen followed of american football, but when I talk to enthusiasts of american football, i come to know the reason why this game is so popular in america is because every player in the team has a role to play, specializes to specific skill and last but not least, the coach who is responsible for strategy plays a pivotal role.

  
  

I hope to see that depending on "PITCH WISDOM INDEX", the cricket team coach has the ability to choose the right team and then on the game day makes some surprising alterations to “check-mate” the rival.
